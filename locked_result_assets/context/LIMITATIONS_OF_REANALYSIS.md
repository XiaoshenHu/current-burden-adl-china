# Limitations of Reanalysis

## Scope note

The Stage 1-11 reanalysis substantially clarified study positioning and claim hierarchy, but it did not remove every remaining methodological uncertainty. The limitations below should continue to be retained in the manuscript or supplementary material.

## Limitations that remain after reanalysis

### 1. No formal external validation within the main CHARLS analysis chain
- Performance comparisons in the main CHARLS workflow remain based on internal validation.
- Stage 3 used honest nested validation, but that still does not substitute for true external validation.

### 2. Phenotype derivation still depended on adversity-complete entry
- The retained phenotype solution still comes from an adversity-complete derivation chain.
- Phenotype construction therefore remains partly influenced by exposure completeness.
- This remains one of the most important unresolved structural limitations.

### 3. Hard phenotype was kept as a 4-class construct for comparability
- Stages 7 and 8 kept the retained 4-class solution as the main comparison target to preserve comparability with the manuscript structure.
- This should not be interpreted as proof that 4 classes would be optimal under every alternative specification.

### 4. Multiple imputation was implemented at the summary level rather than the adversity-item level
- Stage 4 did not perform childhood adversity item-level multiple imputation.
- A summary-level MI strategy was used instead.
- Adversity-related effect estimates should therefore be interpreted mainly for direction and positioning rather than over-read for exact magnitude.

### 5. MI plus IPW is informative but still approximate
- The Stage 4 MI+IPW analysis was not expanded into a fully unrestricted doubly robust framework.
- It is better interpreted as robustness triangulation rather than as a new gold-standard primary estimate.

### 6. Older-adult sensitivity used an approximated wave-3 age
- Stage 10 used `baseline_age_2011 + 4` as an approximation to wave-3 age.
- This is transparent and interpretable, but it is not a directly observed wave-3 age field.

### 7. Some domain-level robustness checks were constrained by available summary variables
- Stage 10 did not have item-level physical limitation components, so original item-count reconstruction was not possible.
- Measurement robustness was therefore assessed using traceable summary-level domain variables.

### 8. A small number of participants could not be fully classified in the Stage 9 multinomial outcome structure
- The main ADL competing-outcome structure included 16 fewer participants than the masked composite sample.
- The supportive IADL competing-outcome structure included 14 fewer participants.
- These participants were alive but their disability status at wave 4 could not be further resolved.

### 9. Clustering robustness was extensive but not exhaustive
- Stage 7 compared multiple covariance structures, used more random starts, and included 80 subsampling iterations.
- This remains a cost-controlled robustness audit rather than an exhaustive search across all possible clustering specifications.

### 10. Physical limitation dominance complicates the meaning of multidomain
- Stage 8 showed that physical limitation was the strongest driving domain.
- The retained phenotype can still be described as multidomain, but more precisely as a multidomain, physically anchored representation.

### 11. The pragmatic rule module remains translational and exploratory
- The Stage R1-R6 pragmatic rule module was developed as a translational extension within the same reanalysis cohort rather than as a preregistered primary analysis module.
- Thresholds for `PTS_6_core` and `HYB_emerging_nonsevere` were derived from the current sample distribution and stability signals rather than external clinical cut-points.
- `HYB_emerging_nonsevere` had low discrimination and is more defensibly interpreted as a selective operational flag than as a general prediction tool.
- Stage R5 did not complete full MI-based nested rule validation.
- The most defensible positioning is therefore exploratory operational aid rather than validated screening instrument.

## Practical implications

Given these limitations, the most defensible manuscript-level claims remain:

- current burden is dominant
- pre-landmark change adds modest independent refinement
- hard phenotype is clinically interpretable but not the strongest predictive representation
- Emerging is a partly burden-independent high-risk phenotype
- adversity is an upstream vulnerability marker rather than a short-horizon mortality predictor

Claims that should still be avoided include:

- hard phenotype clearly improves prediction beyond current burden
- phenotype identifies a fully autonomous pre-disability state
- trajectory is the strongest predictive representation
