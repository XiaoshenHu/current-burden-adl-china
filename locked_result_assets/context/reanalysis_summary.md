# Reanalysis Summary

## Scope

This summary integrates the key locked results across the CHARLS reanalysis stages without adding new model fitting. Its purpose is to state the most defensible scientific interpretation and to clarify which claims should remain central in the manuscript.

## Main integrated conclusions

### 1. Pre-landmark change adds information beyond current burden, but the gain is modest
- In Stage 2, `M2` (base + 4 wave-3 levels + 4 slopes) improved on `M1` (base + 4 wave-3 levels) by `ΔAUC = 0.009 (0.003 to 0.014)`.
- In Stage 3 strict nested validation, the gain remained `ΔAUC = 0.009 (0.004 to 0.014)`.
- This supports writing pre-landmark change as modest incremental refinement rather than major improvement.

### 2. Hard phenotype is weaker than continuous level-plus-slope representation
- Hard phenotype did not outperform the continuous representation in prediction.
- The strongest predictive form remained the explicit level-plus-slope representation, whereas hard phenotype was more useful as a compressed and clinically interpretable subgroup representation.

### 3. Emerging retains information after adjustment for current burden, but not as a fully autonomous state
- In the primary sample, `Emerging vs Stable = 3.866 (3.182, 4.698)`.
- After adjustment for wave-3 current burden in the unified sample, `Emerging vs Stable = 2.258 (1.820, 2.801)`.
- Burden-adjusted absolute risk remained higher for Emerging than for Stable, but the phenotype should still be interpreted as partly burden-independent rather than fully autonomous.

### 4. Trajectory information is better framed as adjunct refinement plus clinical compression
- For prediction, the defensible role is adjunct refinement.
- For explanation, the defensible role is clinically interpretable subgrouping, especially for Emerging multidomain decline.

### 5. The most defensible main manuscript message
- Current multidomain burden at the landmark is the dominant predictor of incident ADL disability.
- Pre-landmark multidomain change provides modest incremental refinement.
- Hard trajectory phenotype is clinically interpretable but not the strongest predictive form.
- Emerging identifies a high-risk, partly burden-independent pre-disability phenotype.
- Childhood adversity is better positioned as an upstream vulnerability marker than as a short-horizon mortality predictor.

## Which stages changed interpretation most?

- Stage 3: strict nested validation
- Stage 4: missingness and selection-bias module
- Stage 5: Emerging beyond current burden
- Stage 8: domain contribution analysis
- Stage 9: disability-versus-mortality pathway structure
